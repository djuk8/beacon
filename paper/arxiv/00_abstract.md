# Abstract

For centers $n_0 = k\,z\#/d$ ($z\#$ a primorial, $d \mid z\#$
even squarefree, $\gcd(k,d)=1$) we determine exactly the average
number of $Q$-rough survivors in windows
$|j| \le (m/2)\ln n_0$: a truncated convolution of even
$d$-smooth integers against primes exceeding $z$ --
an exact finite identity over the residue classes of the
cofactor (Theorem 3.2). The class-thinned channel masses form a
probability distribution (Theorem 3.3): changing the divisor
cannot change the total channel mass, only redistribute it, and
every finite-window advantage of a construction is the mass it
places within reach of the truncation. In the deep-channel limit
the law reproduces, for every admissible $d$, the fence rate
$e^{\gamma}\omega(u)$ previously confirmed independently at
$6$--$8\sigma$ (Corollary 3.4), and the cost exponent is
invariant in the sieve depth (Proposition 3.6). Under two
standard Hardy--Littlewood-type hypotheses this yields a
complete cost law for constructed prime gaps: attempts are
geometric with mean $e^{E}$ (Proposition 2.6). With no fitted parameters the law
reproduces the exact survivor counts of all twenty published
maximal prime gaps of the past decade to $1$--$4\%$, the ring
designs of three record campaigns to $\le 1\%$, and $64$
calibration points spanning $87$ to $385{,}713$ decimal digits.
The practitioners' empirical cost curve is identified as the
shadow of the two-variable law along the record locus; its
slope emerges numerically as $e^{\gamma}$, and we conjecture the
identity (Conjecture 3.9). Applications include placement
optimization, a supply
constraint explaining the stability, since 2017, of the
maximal-merit record, and a measurable
divisibility defect in $3{,}263$ published records, removable by
a one-line coprimality constraint. [PARADE-CONDITIONAL: Records
produced under this law accompany the announcement.]
