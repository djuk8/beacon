# 1. The game
A prime gap is the distance between consecutive primes. Gaps grow
on average like ln p, so raw size mostly reports where you looked;
the community scores by MERIT = gap / ln p1 -- how many average
gaps' worth of nothing, in one stretch. The public record list
(121,457 entries at our snapshot) supports several sports at once:
per-gapsize FIRST-OCCURRENCE records (smallest known start for
each gap), the ALL-TIME MERIT table, and SIZE tables at merit
thresholds ("largest gap with merit >= 20 / >= 30"). Serious
hunters have long used primorial constructions -- centers of the
form k*zd#/d, whose neighborhoods are born mostly empty -- as
folklore; what the folklore lacked was a price. Costs were
discovered by spending them.

This paper's thesis: HOPE IS THE EXPENSIVE PART. The survivor
structure of primorial neighborhoods admits an exact, parameter-
free counting law (Section 2-3), verified against every artifact
we could obtain, including every published record of the decade
(Sections 4-5). With the law, a record hunt becomes a purchase
decision: any target on any board converts to a median cost with
exact quantiles, an optimal placement, and an audit trail
(Section 8). The same law reads inefficiency off published
artifacts (Section 6) and reduces the empirical constant e^gamma
to a stated conjecture about one explicit sum (Section 7).
Everything here is reproducible from a public repository and a
data deposit (Section 9); every claim carries its provenance flag,
and our failed hypotheses ship in Appendix A alongside the
confirmed ones.
