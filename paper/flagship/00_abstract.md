# Abstract
A prime gap of merit m is a run of m*ln p consecutive composites
after a prime p. We give an exact, zero-parameter law for the cost
of producing record gaps by construction: for centers
n0 = k*zd#/d, the expected count of prime candidates in a merit-m
window is a truncated convolution of even d-smooth numbers with
primes beyond zd, computable from Mertens' theorem alone. With no
fitted constants the law reproduces the exact survivor counts of
all twenty published maximal-gap records of the past decade
(1-4%), the complete ring designs of our own three record
campaigns (<=1%), and 64 fresh calibration points spanning 87 to
385,713 digits; its shelf projection explains the empirical cost
curve sigma(u), whose leading coefficient emerges as e^gamma.
Three consequences follow. Every leaderboard acquires a price --
in GPU-days and dollars, as exact medians with quantiles. A decade
of published records carries a measurable design inefficiency of
x1.3-2.4 (3,263 entries, six discoverers), removable by a one-line
coprimality constraint. And the community's deployed compute --
including our own, placed before this law existed -- already sits
on the law's computed minima. [PARADE-CONDITIONAL: Records
produced under this framework, across five leaderboards
simultaneously, accompany this paper.]
